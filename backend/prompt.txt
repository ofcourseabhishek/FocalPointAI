- "overview.keep": the two strongest choices to keep, each with a 1-3 word label and one sentence.
- "overview.change_one_thing": the single highest-impact change. "action" is an imperative

- "frame_work": one entry per category key below. Use "status": "not_applicable" or
  "intentional_absence" (and explain why in "interpretation") when intent_profile says so.
  - "interpretation": one sentence on how this category works in this photograph.
  - "supporting": one sentence of supporting detail or evidence.
  - "metrics": 2-4 specific parts of the category. For each:
    - "label": the part being judged. Prefer these labels so they match the measured evidence:
      composition: Subject placement, Horizon, Leading lines, Crop, Balance
      lighting: Brightness, Highlight clipping, Shadow clipping, Contrast, Direction of light

      visual_hierarchy: Subject separation, Eye path, Distractions
      technical: Noise, Edit quality, Dynamic range

      For a strength, use a positive word: Balanced, Strong, Clean, Sharp, Clear, Natural,
      Even, Pleasing, Effective, Well-placed.
      For a problem, use exactly one of these phrases: Soft focus, Motion blur, Camera shake,
      Noisy, Overexposed, Blown highlights, Underexposed, Crushed shadows, Low contrast, Flat,
      Color cast, Busy background, Competing elements, Crooked horizon, Weak separation,
      Awkward crop, Tight crop.
    - "saw": one sentence on what you see in this exact part of the frame.
    - "matters": one sentence on why it matters to how the photograph reads.
    - For a problem, also "improve" (how to fix it in editing, or say it cannot be fixed in
      editing) and "try" (what to do differently when shooting next time).
    - For a strength, "improve" may suggest how to push it further, or be omitted.
- "aspects", "first_impression" and "suggested_edits" are also used by the PDF report and the
  tutorial recommender: keep them consistent with everything above.
